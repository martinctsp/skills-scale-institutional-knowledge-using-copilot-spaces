# OctoAcme Project Management Docs

Welcome to the OctoAcme Project Management Documentation! This repository contains comprehensive guides and processes for running projects at OctoAcme, from initiation through delivery and continuous improvement.

## Overview

OctoAcme's project management approach is built on five core principles:

- **Customer-first**: Prioritize customer value and usability in every decision
- **Iterative delivery**: Deliver small, testable increments to gather feedback and iterate
- **Clear ownership**: Each project has a named Project Manager (PM) and Product Lead
- **Data-informed decisions**: Measure impact and iterate based on evidence
- **Psychological safety**: Encourage feedback, learning, and continuous improvement

Our methodology guides teams through a complete project lifecycle:

1. **Initiation** — Validate business need, align stakeholders, and confirm go/no-go
2. **Planning** — Define scope, create a prioritized backlog, and establish success metrics
3. **Execution** — Build, test, and deliver in iterative cycles with regular reviews
4. **Release** — Deploy to production with confidence and verify success
5. **Close & Retrospective** — Capture learnings and feed improvements back into our processes

## Key Roles

- **Project Manager (PM)**: Coordinates delivery, manages schedules, risks, and communications
- **Product Manager (PdM)**: Defines outcomes, prioritizes the backlog, and measures success
- **Developers**: Implement features, collaborate on design, and maintain code quality
- **QA/Testing**: Validates quality and acceptance criteria
- **Stakeholders**: Provide inputs, approvals, and guidance

## Communication Cadence

- Daily standups (15 min) — focus on progress, blockers, and dependencies
- Weekly PM + PdM sync — alignment and priority discussion
- Twice-weekly delivery team standups — or as agreed by the team
- Monthly stakeholder updates — progress, risks, and key decisions
- Ad-hoc escalations — when blockers need immediate attention

## Getting Started

**New to OctoAcme projects?**
1. Read the [Project Management Overview](octoacme-project-management-overview.md) for context
2. Explore the role-specific docs relevant to your responsibilities
3. Use the checklists and templates as you move through each project phase

**Starting a new project?**
1. Begin with [Project Initiation](octoacme-project-initiation.md) to validate and authorize the work
2. Move to [Project Planning](octoacme-project-planning.md) once stakeholders approve
3. Follow [Execution and Tracking](octoacme-execution-and-tracking.md) for day-to-day delivery
4. Reference [Risks and Communication](octoacme-risks-and-communication.md) as needed
5. Prepare for release using [Release and Deployment](octoacme-release-and-deployment.md)
6. Close the project with a retrospective using [Retrospective and Continuous Improvement](octoacme-retrospective-and-continuous-improvement.md)

## Key Artifacts

All OctoAcme projects should maintain these artifacts:

- **Project Charter / One-pager** — Problem, goal, success metrics, stakeholders, timeline
- **Roadmap and Release Plan** — High-level feature timeline and key milestones
- **Sprint/Iteration Backlog** — Prioritized, estimated work items with acceptance criteria
- **Definition of Done** — Team agreement on what it means for work to be complete
- **Risk Register** — Ongoing tracking of identified risks, mitigation plans, and status
- **Retrospective Notes** — Action items, learnings, and improvements from completed phases

## Tips for Success

- **Keep documentation updated** — Outdated docs create confusion. Refresh your project charter and risk register weekly.
- **Use templates and checklists** — They're not bureaucracy; they're shortcuts to consistency and completeness.
- **Celebrate improvements** — When an action item reduces cycle time or improves quality, acknowledge it in your next standup.
- **Escalate early** — Use the escalation paths in the risk management guide to surface blockers quickly.
- **Measure and iterate** — Track velocity, burndown, and success metrics to make data-informed decisions.
